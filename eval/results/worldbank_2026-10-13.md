### q2: What does Chapter 3 say about sovereign debt in developing economies?

**hierarchical** — hit: None, score: None 

> Chapter 3 of the document, "A Rising Challenge: Sovereign Debt Levels and Interest Rates in EMDEs," provides the following insights regarding sovereign debt in emerging market and developing economies (EMDEs):
> 
> ### **Debt Trends and Costs**
> *   **Rising Debt Levels:** Aggregate government debt in EMDEs has grown from less than 40 percent of GDP in 2010 to over 70 percent [p. 109, Introduction].
> *   **Increased Servicing Costs:** The cost of servicing this debt has risen from 6 percent of government revenues in 2010 to an estimated 11 percent in 2025 [p. 109, Introduction].
> *   **Impact on Interest Rates:** Debt levels are positively associated with dollar-denominated sovereign bond spreads and domestic-currency government bond yields. The relationship is non-linear, meaning that as debt-to-GDP ratios increase, the associated jumps in interest rates become progressively larger [p. 107].
> *   **Historical Context:** The rise in EMDE debt-to-GDP ratios since 2010 is associated with increases in sovereign spreads of about 110 basis points and domestic-currency yields of about 30 basis points [p. 107].
> 
> ### **Critical Challenges**
> Mounting debt-service payments create four primary risks for EMDEs:
> 1.  **Resource Diversion:** Resources are diverted from priority areas like infrastructure, health, education, and social safety nets, which can hinder productivity and job creation [p. 109, Introduction].
> 2.  **Debt Accumulation:** High debt-service obligations may require additional borrowing, creating a cycle of rising debt [p. 109, Introduction].
> 3.  **Cost of Capital:** Rising sovereign debt costs increase the economy-wide cost of capital, limiting private-sector growth and investment [p. 109, Introduction].
> 4.  **Economic Instability:** This cycle can lead to debt distress, default, and severe economic disruption [p. 109, Introduction].
> 
> ### **Vulnerabilities and Risk Factors**
> *   **Credit Ratings:** EMDEs with weaker credit ratings have experienced larger increases in spreads compared to those with strong ratings [p. 110, Introduction].
> *   **Debt Distress:** The share of low- and middle-income countries in, or at high risk of, debt distress rose from 26 percent in 2015 to about 50 percent in 2026 [p. 110, Introduction].
> *   **Specific Vulnerabilities:** Rising debt leads to larger interest rate increases in countries with a history of default, low credit ratings, frontier market status, heavy reliance on short-term debt, and weak governance [p. 107].
> *   **Hidden Debt:** The revelation of previously undisclosed ("hidden") debt is associated with large, significant, and persistent increases in sovereign spreads [p. 116, Box 3.1].
> 
> ### **Policy Recommendations**
> To strengthen fiscal positions and contain borrowing costs, the chapter suggests:
> *   Stronger domestic revenue mobilization [p. 107].
> *   More efficient public spending [p. 107].
> *   Improved debt management and support for domestic debt market development [p. 107].
> *   Debt-for-development swaps, where appropriate [p. 107].
> *   Improving debt transparency to minimize borrowing costs [p. 116, Box 3.1].
