### q2: What does Chapter 3 say about sovereign debt in developing economies?

**hierarchical** — hit: None, score: None 

> Based on the provided document, Chapter 3 of "Global Economic Prospects, June 2026" addresses the following regarding sovereign debt in emerging market and developing economies (EMDEs):
> 
> **Current Status and Trends**
> *   **Rising Debt Levels:** Aggregate government debt in EMDEs has increased from less than 40 percent of GDP in 2010 to over 70 percent [p. 109, Introduction].
> *   **Increased Debt-Service Costs:** The cost of servicing this debt has risen from 6 percent of government revenues in 2010 to an estimated 11 percent in 2025 [p. 109, Introduction].
> *   **Debt Distress:** The share of low- and middle-income countries either in, or at high risk of, debt distress rose from 26 percent in 2015 to about 50 percent in 2026 [p. 110, Introduction].
> 
> **Impact on Interest Rates**
> *   **Positive Association:** Debt levels are positively associated with dollar-denominated sovereign bond spreads and domestic-currency government bond yields [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   **Non-linear Relationship:** The relationship is non-linear; increases in debt-to-GDP ratios lead to greater increases in spreads or yields the higher the initial debt-to-GDP ratio is [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   **Quantified Impact:** The rise in EMDE debt-to-GDP ratios since 2010 is associated with increases in sovereign spreads of about 110 basis points and domestic-currency yields of about 30 basis points [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   **Vulnerabilities:** Rising debt leads to larger interest rate increases in countries with a history of default, low credit ratings, frontier market status, heavy reliance on short-term debt, and weak governance [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> 
> **Critical Challenges**
> Mounting debt-service payments create four primary challenges:
> 1.  **Diversion of Resources:** Resources are diverted from priority areas like infrastructure, health, education, and social safety nets, which can hinder productivity and job creation [p. 109, Introduction].
> 2.  **Self-Reinforcing Debt:** High debt-service obligations may require additional borrowing, causing debt to rise further [p. 109, Introduction].
> 3.  **Increased Cost of Capital:** Rising sovereign debt costs increase the economy-wide cost of capital, limiting private-sector growth and investment [p. 109, Introduction].
> 4.  **Economic Disruption:** The cycle can trigger debt distress, default, and severe economic disruption [p. 109, Introduction].
> 
> **Policy Recommendations**
> To strengthen fiscal positions and contain borrowing costs, the chapter suggests:
> *   Stronger domestic revenue mobilization [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   More efficient public spending [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   Improved debt management [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   Support for domestic debt market development [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   Debt-for-development swaps, where appropriate [p. 107, Chapter 3].
> *   Improving debt transparency, as hidden debt is associated with significantly higher borrowing costs [p. 116, Box 3.1].
